# Group A redirect migration notes — 2026-08-19

These notes preserve the useful distinctions from the six Tier-B source pages before their URLs are permanently consolidated. They are not publication-ready copy. The canonical destination should absorb these ideas only where they add information and do not duplicate stronger material.

## R-01 `/blog/open-web-sourcing-stack/` → `/blog/linkedin-recruiter-alternatives/`

Preserve:
- Open-web sourcing should be organized as independent evidence surfaces rather than one generic web-search lane.
- Role-to-surface examples: GitHub for public technical evidence; OpenAlex or Semantic Scholar for research evidence; professional registries for healthcare; contact finders for professional contact paths; ATS rediscovery for owned history.
- Discovery and contact enrichment are separate jobs and should remain separate in the replacement-stack framework.
- Open-web does not mean unrestricted collection: platform rules, privacy, opt-outs, and manual-source boundaries still apply.

## R-02 `/blog/sourcing-tool-stack-for-agency-recruiters/` → `/blog/linkedin-recruiter-alternatives/`

Preserve for an agency/small-team audience section:
- Agency constraints emphasize speed, credit economics, bounce risk, and repeatability.
- A practical lightweight stack can combine BooleanOS, X-Ray, a domain-specific evidence surface, one contact finder, email verification, CRM notes, and a repeatable source pack.
- Compare contact-credit cost against evidence-fit yield and downstream outreach outcomes, not lookup volume alone.
- Evidence should precede enrichment; speed should not erase relevance or opt-out discipline.

## R-03 `/blog/sourcing-for-founders-and-small-teams/` → `/blog/linkedin-recruiter-alternatives/`

Preserve for a founder/small-team audience section:
- Build the search system before buying a large stack.
- Minimum workflow: role intake, Boolean, X-Ray, donor-company map, contact policy, and simple search-lane tracking.
- A founder's personalization advantage only helps after the search has narrowed to plausible people.
- Buy contact data after search strategy is producing useful evidence, not as a substitute for role clarity.

## R-04 `/blog/hard-to-fill-role-intake-template/` → `/blog/senior-sourcer-role-intake/`

Preserve:
- Intake fields should explicitly capture role outcome, must-have evidence, title flexibility, location rules, clearance/license requirements, disqualifiers, compensation, donor companies, and review criteria.
- Tradeoff fields should force a decision about what can flex: title, years, domain, location, tech stack, compensation, industry, or clearance crossover.
- Verification fields should list what must be confirmed manually, including clearance status, license, onsite eligibility, work authorization, recency of skill, and current interest.

## R-05 `/blog/hiring-manager-calibration-questions/` → `/blog/senior-sourcer-role-intake/`

Preserve:
- Sequence the meeting: define success, define evidence, define what can flex, then review what profiles have failed before.
- Technical calibration should separate truly required tools from interchangeable technologies and ask what project context proves the skill.
- Cleared-role calibration should distinguish required clearance level, crossover rules, location/onsite constraints, and information that cannot be discussed publicly.

## R-06 `/blog/govcon-cleared-sourcing-market-map/` → `/blog/where-to-find-cleared-candidates/`

Preserve:
- GovCon sourcing is a market-map problem before it is a keyword problem.
- Separate defense primes, intelligence contractors, cloud vendors, systems integrators, cyber firms, and mission-tech startups rather than treating GovCon as one donor pool.
- Search mission/program language and secure-delivery context such as RMF, ATO, NIST, FedRAMP, GovCloud, ICD, SCIF, polygraph, and agency context carefully.
- Public clearance language remains a breadcrumb; current status belongs in the authorized verification process.

Exact Search Console sessions, query counts, and referring-domain data were not available through the connected sources during this implementation pass. Public search discovery on 2026-08-19 confirmed that the Tier-B cohort is discoverable from the SourcingOS blog index and that the GovCon market-map URL itself had been crawled/indexed. The permanent redirects preserve URL equity while internal links and sitemap entries are moved directly to the canonical destinations.
